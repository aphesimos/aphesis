A picture made only of fog: an image becomes a density field and coalesces out of the stipple.

- **Provide** a canvas and a source: a grayscale density map URL (white = dense), an image or canvas element, or `Aphesis.Simulacrum.fromBrush(draw, w, h)` for a symbol drawn with the brush.
- `Aphesis.Simulacrum(canvas, {source, fit: 'contain' | 'cover', gain, scatter, erosion, ceiling, cell, accent, margin, ground})` returns `{show(src?, done), hide(done), set(src, done), stop(), start(), drawn(cb)}`. `show` coalesces in 2.6s, `hide` dissolves in 1.8s, `set` dissolves, swaps and coalesces. The options object stays live: change `gain`, `scatter`, `erosion`, `ceiling`, `accent`, `margin` or `fit` on it and the next frame follows.
- **Over the fog**, pass `ground: false`: everything but the cells is transparent, so the picture lies over the fog canvas with no blend mode, and an undrawn canvas shows nothing. Blend modes on WebGL canvases flash during theme changes and resizes.
- **Reuse canvases.** Make the simulacra a screen needs once and give them one picture after another (`hide`, then `show(next)`); never make and throw away canvases or contexts per picture. `stop()` rests a hidden one, `start()` wakes it, `drawn(cb)` says when its next frame is on screen: keep the canvas at opacity 0 until then. A lost WebGL context is rebuilt with its picture when the browser restores it.
- `scatter` (.5) lets lost points drift around the figure, `erosion` (.45) opens fog-shaped holes in it, `ceiling` (.8) caps how full any area gets. A source may carry its own: `{src, scatter, erosion, ceiling, gain}`. Sculpture on a plain ground: `{erosion: .5, scatter: .85, ceiling: .76}`.
- **Use** for the experimental chambers, section openings and any picture. Caption every one with work, artist and date in `meta`.
- **Do** prepare maps as the README's Simulacra rules say (dark grounds, lit masses, crops without frames, ≤ 640px). **Don't** set `accent` above .05.

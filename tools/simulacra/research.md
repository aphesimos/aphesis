# Lit bodies on black make the best simulacra

The images most likely to render the way *Creation of Adam*, the Siena Hermes and the Flammarion engraving did share one structure: **a large, modelled, light-coloured mass on a ground that is already near-black**, so the renderer's dots gather into volume and fade into the void without any help. Three families supply nearly all of them. The first is the **other Siena pavement panels** (the Libyan and Erythraean Sibyls, the Hill of Wisdom), which come from the same PD scan series as the Hermes that worked. The second is **tenebrist and Michelangelesque paintings** in which lit flesh crosses a black field: Caravaggio's *Saint Jerome Writing*, Blake's *Newton*, Ribera's *Ixion* and Wright of Derby's *Alchemist*. The third is **single-light-source cosmic prints** in the Flammarion line: Doré's Empyrean, John Martin's *Creation of Light*, Bosch's tunnel. The shortlist below ranks 36 works by fit with the user's taste. It puts the Creation-of-Adam-like and Siena-like works first and the Flammarion-like radiant compositions next, and sends works on paper that need inversion or blurring to the bottom. For the ouroboros, the answer is to stop looking at alchemical manuscripts: the snake renders best as **an attribute inside a chiaroscuro oil painting** (Cagnacci, Giulio Romano) or a densely hatched Merian-school plate, never as a flat coloured ring. Treat every ranking as a hypothesis. **None of these images has been run through the aphes.is renderer**. All judgments come from the researchers' reading of tonal structure and from greyscale thumbnails. File names, pixel sizes and licence tags were read from the Wikimedia Commons API on 2026-11-10.

## Why the four failures and the four successes point to one rule

The successes and failures separate cleanly once you ask what the density map sees. The Siena Hermes is **pale marble figures with dark graffito lines on a dark inlaid ground** ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pavimento_di_siena,_ermete_trismegisto_(giovanni_di_stefano)_01.jpg)). *Creation of Adam* is lit, sculptural flesh. Flammarion has one dominant light zone set against a large dark field. In all three, luminance on its own already describes a form with depth. The failures each break this in a different way. The Chrysopoeia ouroboros and the brush-drawn monad are thin lines on a blank ground, so the map holds almost no area. Khunrath's laboratory is uniform hatching, which turns into an even, "ascii"-like dot texture. The Praxiteles photo sits in a narrow mid-grey band against a busy, pale museum background. The researchers converge on the same diagnosis from different directions: "one dominant light source plus large dark field" for prints, "lit volume on plaster" for paintings, and "big mass plus detail" for mosaics.

This report assumes the renderer's polarity is **bright pixel = dense dots**, which is consistent with the Hermes result. "Luminance direct" means feeding the greyscale image unchanged. "Invert" applies to dark-ink-on-light-paper sources where the ink is the subject. The notes disagree on one case. The paintings researcher suggested inverting Rembrandt's *Faust* etching. The prints researcher said to map light to dots, because the radiant disc is the white paper. With bright = dense, the second reading is correct: inverting would empty the disc. The shortlist follows it.

On licensing, the coordinator dropped the public-domain requirement. Nearly everything is still PD or CC0 anyway, because the artists died long ago. The exceptions are museum-floor photographs under CC BY or CC BY-SA. The mosaics researcher points out that a stippled recreation of a CC BY-SA photo is arguably a derivative work, so attribution or share-alike may still apply. PD alternatives are named where they exist.

## Better ouroboros depictions live inside paintings, not manuscripts

The Chrysopoeia failed because it is the archetype of the whole manuscript tradition: a flat ring with script around and inside it. Its relatives repeat the problem. These include the Pelecanos copy of 1478 (`Serpiente alquimica.jpg`, 625×621), whose red and green bands convert to near-equal grey so the body drops out, the *Aurora consurgens* dragon-pot, the 18th-century "Arabic" ring, the *Clavis Artis* wash drawing and the Cyprianus diagram ([Ouroboros category](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Ouroboros)). The fix is to reverse the hierarchy. Choose a picture whose volume reads first, with the snake as a detail found on a second look. That is also exactly the "slightly hard to decode" quality the user wants.

**Cagnacci's *Allegoria della vita umana*** (rank 6) is the best ouroboros source found: a nude in chiaroscuro on black with the snake ring as a halo ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Allegoria_della_vita_umana_(Guido_Cagnacci))). Its one weakness is that the ring is thin, so raise contrast before rendering. **Giulio Romano's *Allegory of Immortality*** (rank 25) sets a small ouroboros among phoenix and cloud masses, with the cosmic mood of Flammarion; Commons also has detail crops (`Giulio romano, allegoria dell'immortalità, 1540 ca. 02–05`) ([category](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Allegoria_dell'immortalità_(Giulio_Romano,_1540))). **Luca Giordano's *Cave of Eternity*** has a large ouroboros coiling across a dark cave mouth full of lit figures, but the only Commons file is **698×581** (`Giordano, Luca - Die Höhle der Ewigkeit.jpg`, PD), so a better scan is needed before it is usable ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Giordano,_Luca_-_Die_H%C3%B6hle_der_Ewigkeit.jpg)).

Among true alchemical emblems, the Merian-school engravings beat the painted manuscripts because their hatching is dense enough to act as tone, unlike Khunrath's light, even lines. **Maier's *Atalanta Fugiens* Emblem 14** ("Hic est Draco caudam suam devorans") shows a dark winged dragon in front of masonry. The handy file is only 630×550 ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Michael_Maier_Atalanta_Fugiens_Emblem_14.jpeg)). The Science History Institute's 4245×5169 page scans of the book are on Commons, but which page holds Emblem 14 was not identified ([DPLA scan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Atalanta_Fugiens_-_DPLA_-_010d4b4a0bc98218f8323bbd7cb8973c_(page_75).jpg)). The **1625 *Dyas chymica tripartita* wyvern** (`Ouroboros 1.jpg`, 815×832, PD) coils its pale body against dark foliage ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ouroboros_1.jpg)). **Lambspring's "Sexta Figura"** (`Musaeum Hermeticum 1678 p 353 Dragon.png`, 1725×1700, PD) has a tonal woodland, but its dragon is small and mid-grey ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Musaeum_Hermeticum_1678_p_353_Dragon_0049.JPG)). For all three, crop out motto and frame, invert so the dark dragon becomes the dense mass, and blur 2–4 px. Of the three, the wyvern is the strongest bet for silhouette.

The serpent-of-time sculptures form a third route. The **Vatican and Sidon leontocephalines** (ranks 8 and 30) are snake-coiled Aion figures on dark grounds, and arguably better simulacra of "eternity devouring itself" than any drawn ring. One lead is open: `Sebastiano Ricci 003.jpg` (2536×3237, PD) sits in Category:Ouroboros and is a dark, strongly chiaroscuro Baroque canvas. Its title and the snake's position were not identified, so check it visually.

## Six preparation rules distilled from the findings

**First, choose by ground, not by subject.** A near-black ground (tenebrist oils, the Siena inlay, the Galla Placidia dome, sculpture shot against black cloth) is the only reliable way to get the void around the dots. Everything else needs work, and work loses information. **Second, use luminance directly whenever the subject is light.** That covers oils, mosaics, marble and radiant prints, the Faust disc included. Invert only paper works where dark ink *is* the subject (the Redon black sun, Merian dragons). When in doubt, as with Redon's eye or Baldung, render both polarities.

**Third, clamp the light source.** A candle, lamp or phosphorus flask is the brightest pixel and will saturate into a solid blob, so cap it with a gamma of about 1.3 or a highlight mask. **Fourth, blur any hatching before mapping.** A Gaussian blur at 2–4 times the dot pitch turns engraved line into tone; this is the most likely fix for the Khunrath "ascii" failure. It does not rescue thin outline art, which simply has no area. **Fifth, crush mid-grey grounds.** Gold mosaic grounds, Symbolist skies, flaking frescoes and grey museum walls fill with dots unless the levels clip them below roughly 40–45%. If they share a tonal band with the subject, isolate the subject with a mask instead. **Sixth, convert colour with care.** Red and green, yellow and red, or blue and green can collapse to the same grey. Preview a luminance conversion before choosing, and use weighted channels or posterize to keep the steps (Grünewald's halo, the Pelecanos ring). Mezzotints and lithographs can work against you by being too soft. Add a contrast curve rather than reaching for a line source.

## What to avoid

| Avoid | Example from the notes | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Flat-colour manuscript ouroboroi | Chrysopoeia; Pelecanos 1478; *Aurora consurgens*; "Arabic" ring; Cyprianus | Thin outline, no modelling; script becomes noise; colours collapse in greyscale |
| Pure burin and line engravings or woodcuts | Khunrath; Dürer *Melencolia I* and *Saint Jerome*; Fludd; Kircher uncoloured; Lévi's *Baphomet*; Blake *Job* pl. 14 | Even hatching turns into uniform dot texture; usable only cropped to a dark zone and heavily blurred |
| Gold-ground or pale-ground icons and frescoes | Hagia Sophia Deësis; Villa of the Mysteries (cinnabar goes mid-grey); Isiac ceremony | A narrow mid-grey band, the Praxiteles failure in mosaic form |
| Light papyri and outline Egyptian art | *Weighing of the Heart* (Ani); Nut papyrus | Light ground and outline figures give a sparse, flat result even inverted |
| In-situ sculpture with busy pale surroundings | Hermes Ludovisi in situ; Bernini *Teresa* uncropped; Corradini *Veiled Christ* on mid-grey | The background competes with the figure, the Praxiteles failure exactly |
| Flat or mid-key Symbolism | Khnopff *I Lock My Door*; Schwabe *Death and the Gravedigger*; Redon *Cyclops*; Blake *Ghost of a Flea* | No blacks, so the dots spread evenly |
| Over-dark, near-empty fields | Stuck *Luzifer*; Fludd's black square ("too flat"); Doré *Inferno* 34 | Too little bright area; the field reads as sparse noise |
| Low-resolution Commons copies | Delville *Trésors de Satan* (1812×1748) and *Orphée* (599×1600); Giordano *Cave* (698×581); Torcello *Last Judgment* (445×760) | Not enough detail for interior decoding; find a better scan first |

## Conclusion

The user's taste comes down to a technical criterion: the image must already *be* a density map, with tone doing the drawing and a ground that is already the renderer's black. That explains why a 15th-century marble floor and a Sistine ceiling succeed while famous alchemical emblems fail. It also changes how to search. The most esoteric-looking source is often the worst, while Baroque devotional and vanitas painting, which is not hermetic at all on its face, carries the skulls, flames, serpents and divine light the site needs in exactly the right tonal form. The cheapest next step is a test batch of six images covering the families: Libyan Sibyl, *Saint Jerome Writing*, Blake's *Newton*, Wright's *Alchemist*, Doré's *Paradiso* 31 and the Cagnacci. Those six results would confirm the polarity assumption and calibrate the clamp and crush values before the remaining 30 are prepared. Two caveats remain.

Hermes Trismegistus, thrice-great Hermes, is the name under which the Greeks of Egypt joined their Hermes to Thoth, the Egyptian god of writing and reckoning. a body of texts gathered under the name: the Greek treatises now called the *Corpus Hermeticum* and, later and in Arabic, the Emerald Tablet, the short text the alchemists read as their charter.

in 1463 Marsilio Ficino set aside his translation of Plato to put the *Corpus* into Latin for Cosimo de' Medici, who wanted to read it before he died. it was taken for the wisdom of a sage older than Moses. in 1614 Isaac Casaubon showed from its language that it was written in the first centuries after Christ.

the line this site leans on comes from the Tablet:

> that which is below is like that which is above, and that which is above is like that which is below, to do the miracles of one only thing.
>
> — Isaac Newton's translation, c. 1680

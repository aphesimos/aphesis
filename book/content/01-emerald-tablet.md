The Emerald Tablet is a text of barely a dozen sentences attributed to Hermes Trismegistus, the "thrice-great" sage in whom Greek writers merged Hermes with the Egyptian Thoth. Its earliest known version is Arabic, embedded in a book on the secrets of creation ascribed to Balīnūs, a name for Apollonius of Tyana.

Translated into Latin in the twelfth century, it became a founding text of European alchemy. Hortulanus commented on it in the fourteenth century, and Isaac Newton made his own English translation. Its lines describe a single substance that rises from earth to heaven and descends again, and their most quoted phrase, usually shortened to "as above, so below", states the correspondence between the heavens and the earth.

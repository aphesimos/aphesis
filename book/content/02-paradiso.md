Paradiso is the third and last part of Dante's Divine Comedy: thirty-three cantos in terza rima. Guided by Beatrice, the poet rises through the nine spheres of the Ptolemaic heavens, from the Moon to the Primum Mobile, and passes beyond them into the Empyrean, which lies outside space and time.

There the blessed appear seated in the form of an immense white rose, the image Gustave Doré engraved for his illustrated edition of 1868. In the last cantos Bernard of Clairvaux takes Beatrice's place as guide, and the poem closes on a vision of God as three circles of light, where Dante's power to speak fails and his will is turned by the love named in the final line.

Jerome, who lived from about 347 to 420, spent much of his life in a monastery in Bethlehem translating the Bible from Hebrew and Greek into Latin. His translation, later called the Vulgate, was the Bible of the Latin West for more than a thousand years.

Caravaggio painted him for Cardinal Scipione Borghese, and the canvas is still in the Galleria Borghese in Rome. The old man, wrapped in red, reads intently while his right arm stretches across the table to dip his pen. On the books at the other end lies a skull, which repeats the shape of his bald head: a memento mori set beside the work of a scholar, in a dark room lit from one side.

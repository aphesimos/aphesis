Rembrandt's etching shows a scholar who has risen from his desk and turns toward a window, where a radiant disc of light hangs in the air. Letters circle the disc, with INRI at its centre, and a half-seen hand points to a mirror beneath it. Rembrandt left no title; the name Faust became attached to the print after his death, and it has stayed.

The legend of the doctor who trades his soul for knowledge had circulated since the sixteenth century, in the chapbook of 1587 and in Marlowe's play. Goethe used a copy of this etching as the frontispiece of Faust, ein Fragment in 1790, and the scholar facing the apparition has since been read as his Faust at the moment of conjuring.

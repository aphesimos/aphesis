Newton is one of William Blake's twelve large colour prints, made in 1795 by printing colour from a board onto paper and finishing each impression by hand in ink and watercolour. The copy dated around 1805 is in the Tate collection.

Isaac Newton sits naked on a rock covered with growth, apparently at the bottom of the sea, bent over a diagram that he measures with a pair of compasses. His body is drawn with the force of a Michelangelo figure, yet his gaze stays fixed on the geometry at his feet. For Blake, Newton stood for "single vision": a reason that measures the world and in doing so stops seeing it. The print is often read beside its companion, Nebuchadnezzar.

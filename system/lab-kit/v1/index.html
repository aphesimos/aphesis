the lab kit

one stylesheet and one optional script, so that every viewer of the laboratory reads as one body of work: the two faces, the palette, the stipple where it helps, and charts that stay readable.

use it

link the stylesheet, put class="lab" on the body, and add the script if you want the theme switch, the stipple ground, stippled bands and tooltips. the version is in the path: v1 only ever changes in ways that keep a page working.

<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://aphes.ist/lab-kit/v1/lab.css">
<script src="https://aphes.ist/lab-kit/v1/lab.js" defer></script>   <!-- optional -->

readability first

nothing here moves unless asked. the stipple ground is off until a reader turns it on, and still when it is on; charts draw once. the system's request for reduced motion is followed.

colour

the categorical hues are pigments held to rubedo's discipline: the same lightness band and a moderate chroma, ordered so that neighbours stay apart for every kind of colour vision. they start at two series; one series is ink. magnitude is the density of ink; polarity runs from lapis to rubedo through the grey of the ground. the numbers are the contrast on this ground, measured in the page.

        the receding header

        this page's header stays while the page scrolls, and what passes under it recedes instead of being cut: it is faint under most of the header and comes clear just past it, through the same band as the site's. the veil is the ground's own colour, so there is no bar to see; a faint blur sits under it where the browser can draw one, and goes when the stipple ground is on, so the cells never smear.

        do

        keep the header short (one line, the nav on a second line on a phone), let the page itself scroll, and leave the blur optional. links to a part of the page land below the header on their own.

        don't

        give the header a fill, a border or a shadow, raise the blur past a few pixels, or blur the stipple ground. if a reader cannot read what is under the header, it has receded enough.

        point clouds

        points are single square cells, like the fog: they do not grow with nearness, and the far ones sink into the ground instead. a cluster in focus is drawn at two cells and the rest recede; the one picked point is red. drag, or focus the picture and use the arrow keys, to turn it.

The engraving first appeared in L'atmosphère: météorologie populaire, a book on the air and the weather by the astronomer Camille Flammarion, published in Paris in 1888. Its artist is unknown. It imitates the style of a sixteenth-century woodcut, and for a long time it was taken to be one.

A traveller with a staff kneels at the edge of the world, where the starry vault meets the ground, and pushes his head and one arm through it. Beyond the sky he sees wheels within wheels, suns and bands of cloud: the machinery that moves the heavens. In Flammarion's book it illustrates a medieval belief in a solid firmament; it has since become an image of the wish to see past the limits of what is known.
